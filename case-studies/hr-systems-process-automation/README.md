# HR Systems & Process Automation: Digitising 27 HR Processes on a Cloud HRIS

> **Summary:** Gathered the user requirements, designed the process flows and configured a cloud HR information system that now runs about 27 HR processes end to end, replacing manual, paper and email-based workflows across a financial services group, and owning the system's maintenance, support and vendor relationship afterwards.

`HRIS` `Requirements gathering` `Process automation` `System configuration` `UAT` `Data migration` `Change management` · **Domain:** Digital transformation · HR operations · **Role:** People Data Analyst · **Period:** Feb 2023 – present

---

## 1. Overview
A financial services group ran its HR processes manually: forms, emails and spreadsheets moving between employees, line managers and the HR division. I worked with HR process owners to define what each process needed to do, translated that into requirements and workflows in a cloud HR information system, configured and tested them, migrated the employee data, trained users, and have since owned the system's day-to-day maintenance, user support and vendor liaison.

The same system is the source of the data behind my [HR & Workforce Analytics Suite](../hr-workforce-analytics/) case study, so this project also fixed data quality at the point of capture rather than downstream.

## 2. Business Problem
- HR processes ran on forms, email and spreadsheets, so requests were chased rather than tracked
- Turnaround depended on who was available to move paperwork along
- Employees and line managers could not see the status of their own requests, or serve themselves
- Employee data was captured inconsistently across processes, which weakened every downstream report

## 3. Objective
- Move HR processes off manual workflows and onto a single system
- Give employees and managers self-service access to the processes that concern them
- Capture HR data once, consistently, so reporting could be trusted
- Keep the system running reliably after go-live, rather than handing it over and walking away

## 4. Scope: processes automated
Around **27 HR processes** now run on the system, spanning the employee lifecycle:

| Area | Examples |
|---|---|
| Leave & absence | Requests, approvals, balances |
| Onboarding & offboarding | New hire setup, exits and clearance |
| Employee records & self-service | Personal data, documents, employee-raised requests |
| Performance management | Goal setting, review cycles, appraisal ratings |
| Talent management | Talent review and succession processes |
| Learning & development | Training requests, attendance and records |
| Recruitment | Requisitions and applicant tracking |
| Payroll inputs | Approved changes feeding the payroll process |
| Employee engagement | Volunteering and other group-wide programmes |

## 5. My Role & Approach

1. **Requirements gathering** — Worked with HR process owners to document how each process actually runs, who approves what, and what the system needed to enforce.
2. **Process design** — Redesigned manual steps as system workflows: forms, approval chains, notifications and status tracking, rather than lifting the paper process as-is.
3. **Configuration** — Configured the modules, workflows and approval routes in the HRIS.
4. **Data migration & clean-up** — Prepared and loaded employee data into the system, resolving inconsistencies before they became permanent.
5. **Testing / UAT** — Tested each workflow before release and coordinated user acceptance testing with the process owners.
6. **Training & adoption** — Trained HR users and supported employees and managers through the change to self-service.
7. **Maintenance & support** — Own day-to-day support within the HR division: troubleshooting issues, handling configuration changes as policies evolve, and logging, tracking and escalating fixes with the vendor.
8. **Reporting** — Extract and model the system's data for HR reporting and dashboards (see the workforce analytics case study).

## 6. Tools & Technologies

## 7. Business Impact

**From manual to automated** — Around 27 HR processes that previously ran on forms, email and spreadsheets now run in the system, with defined approval routes and an audit trail on every request.

**Faster turnaround, less admin** — Requests route to the right approver automatically instead of being chased, and HR spends less time moving paperwork and answering "where is my request?".

**Self-service adoption** — Employees and line managers now raise and track their own requests, rather than routing everything through the HR division.

**Better data quality at source** — Capturing HR data once, in a structured system, removed a large share of the inconsistency that used to surface during reporting. The workforce dashboards are built on this foundation.

**A system that stays supported** — Ongoing maintenance, user support and vendor escalation keep the processes working as policies change, instead of the configuration drifting out of date.

## 9. Challenges & Solutions

| Challenge | Type | Solution |
|---|---|---|
| Processes existed as habit and email chains rather than documented flows | Business | Worked through each one with its owner and documented it before configuring anything |
| Automating a broken process just makes it faster | Business / Process | Redesigned steps and approval routes during requirements rather than replicating the manual version |
| Employee data arriving inconsistent from manual records | Technical | Cleaned and standardised data before migration, then relied on the system's structure to keep it consistent |
| Users accustomed to sending forms by email | Change management | Training, hands-on support and a self-service path that was quicker than the old route |
| Policies change, so configuration goes stale | Operational | Own the change requests and vendor escalations so the system keeps matching current policy |

## 10. Lessons Learned
- **Technical:** structure at the point of capture beats cleaning downstream; every field the system enforces is a data-quality problem you never have to fix in a pipeline.
- **Business:** requirements gathering is the project. Configuration is fast once the process is genuinely understood, and slow when it isn't.
- **Operational:** a system launch is the start of the work, not the end of it. Support and vendor management are what keep adoption alive.

## 11. Future Improvements
- Deeper reporting and alerting on process turnaround times, so bottlenecks are visible
- Tighter integration between the HRIS data and the analytics layer
- Reviewing the remaining manual HR processes for automation
